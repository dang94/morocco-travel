PRACTICAL GUIDE

Navigating the Dirham

The Moroccan Dirham (MAD) is a closed currency, meaning you can't easily buy or sell it outside of Morocco. Mastering the exchange and understanding where to use cash versus cards is essential for a seamless journey through the medinas and beyond.

Riad Tipping

In Riads, tipping is a gesture of appreciation for the intimate service. A general rule is 50-100 MAD per day for the staff, usually left in a common tip box or given to the manager at checkout.

SUGGESTED: 10% of total stay

Souk Exchange Rates

While ATMs are widely available in New Cities (Ville Nouvelle), for the best rates in the Souks, look for small exchange kiosks near the main gates like Bab Boujloud. They often offer better rates than hotels or airport counters.

PRO TIP: Cash vs. Card

Morocco is still very much a cash-heavy society. While luxury hotels and high-end restaurants in Marrakech or Casablanca will gladly accept your Visa or Mastercard, 90% of your souk purchases, taxi rides, and street food delights will require physical Dirhams. Always keep at least 500 MAD in small bills on you for daily expenses.
